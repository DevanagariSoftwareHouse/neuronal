\chapter{Tensors, reduced-rank models, and inverse problems}\label{ch:inverse}
\begin{readergoal}Move from matrix factorization to multivariate measurements, and learn how identifiable models differ from underdetermined ones.\end{readergoal}
\section{More than two indices}
An experiment with neurons $n$, time $t$, and trial $r$ may be stored as a third-order tensor $\mathcal X_{ntr}$. Its modes represent different axes of variation. A CANDECOMP/PARAFAC (CP) approximation is
\[\mathcal X_{ntr}\approx\sum_{k=1}^K a_{nk}b_{tk}c_{rk}.\]
Interpretation requires confronting scaling and permutation ambiguities: multiplying one factor by $\alpha$ and dividing another by $\alpha$ leaves the product unchanged. Additional uniqueness conditions depend on the factors and rank. The mathematical pattern is broader than one physiological explanation.

\section{Reduced-rank regression}
For predictor matrix $X\in\mathbb R^{T\times p}$ and response matrix $Y\in\mathbb R^{T\times q}$, ordinary multivariate regression fits $Y\approx XB$. Reduced-rank regression requires $\rank(B)\le r$, controlling the dimension of predictive communication. Calling the resulting dimensions ``communication subspaces'' is an interpretation built on the predictive model, not proof that specific axons mediate the influence.

\section{The forward model is not the inverse model}
Let measurements obey $y=Hx+\varepsilon$, where $x$ is a source, $H$ a measurement operator, and $\varepsilon$ noise. The \emph{forward problem} computes $y$ from a chosen source and measurement physics. The \emph{inverse problem} estimates $x$ from $y$. If $H$ has a nontrivial kernel, $x$ and $x+z$ generate the same noiseless measurement whenever $Hz=0$. This is an identifiability failure, not a limitation remedied merely by better numerical precision.

For extracellular electric potentials, source geometry and volume conduction affect $H$, and several current patterns may explain the same electrode measurements. Biophysical treatments emphasize the interpretation of forward models and the assumptions behind inverse methods \cite{Buzsaki2012,Einevoll2013}. Regularization can choose one plausible solution, but it cannot magically restore missing independent information.

\section{Regularized reconstruction}
One possible estimator is $\hat x=\arg\min_x\{\|y-Hx\|_2^2+\lambda\|Lx\|_2^2\}$. If the normal equations yield an invertible matrix, $\hat x=(H^\top H+\lambda L^\top L)^{-1}H^\top y$. The operator $L$ expresses a prior preference (e.g., smoothness), while $\lambda$ sets its influence. The result answers a conditional question: which sources fit the observations well while obeying the chosen regularity assumption?

\section{The Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse}
Let $X=U\Sigma V^\top$ with $r$ nonzero singular values. The \emph{pseudoinverse} is $X^+=V\Sigma^+U^\top$, where $\Sigma^+$ inverts the nonzero singular values and transposes the shape.

\begin{theorem}
For any $y$, the vector $\hat\beta=X^+y$ is the minimum-norm solution among all minimizers of $\|y-X\beta\|_2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the coordinates $\gamma=V^\top\beta$ and $c=U^\top y$, orthogonality gives $\|y-X\beta\|^2=\sum_{j\le r}(c_j-\sigma_j\gamma_j)^2+\sum_{j>r}c_j^2$. The minimizers are exactly the $\gamma$ with $\gamma_j=c_j/\sigma_j$ for $j\le r$ and arbitrary $\gamma_j$ for $j>r$. Since $\|\beta\|=\|\gamma\|$, the smallest-norm choice sets $\gamma_j=0$ for $j>r$, which is $\hat\beta=V\Sigma^+U^\top y$.
\end{proof}
The theorem identifies the arbitrary choice made by a pseudoinverse: components of $\beta$ in $\ker X$ are invisible to the data and are set to zero. In source localization, this ``minimum-norm estimate'' favors superficial sources, because deep sources need larger currents to produce the same scalp or electrode field; depth weighting changes the norm and therefore the answer.

\section{Ill-posedness and the Picard condition}
Expand the noiseless data $y=Xx$ in the singular basis: $c_j=u_j^\top y=\sigma_j(v_j^\top x)$. The pseudoinverse estimate is
\[
\hat x=\sum_{j\le r}\frac{u_j^\top y}{\sigma_j}v_j.
\]
With noise $y=Xx+\varepsilon$, the term for component $j$ contains $u_j^\top\varepsilon/\sigma_j$. When singular values decay towards zero, as they do for smoothing operators such as volume conduction, the noise is divided by tiny numbers and dominates. A problem is \emph{well posed} in Hadamard's sense if a solution exists, is unique, and depends continuously on the data; inverse problems in electrophysiology typically fail the second or third condition. The discrete \emph{Picard condition} asks that $|u_j^\top y|$ decay faster than $\sigma_j$, so that the true signal is recoverable at least in the leading components.

\section{Tikhonov regularization through the SVD}
With $L=I$, the Tikhonov estimate $\hat x_\lambda=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y$ becomes
\[
\hat x_\lambda=\sum_j\frac{\sigma_j^2}{\sigma_j^2+\lambda}\,\frac{u_j^\top y}{\sigma_j}v_j.
\]
The \emph{filter factors} $\phi_j=\sigma_j^2/(\sigma_j^2+\lambda)$ are near $1$ for $\sigma_j^2\gg\lambda$ and near $0$ for $\sigma_j^2\ll\lambda$. Regularization is therefore a soft truncation of poorly determined components. \emph{Truncated SVD} uses $\phi_j\in\{0,1\}$ instead. Choosing $\lambda$ (by cross-validation, generalized cross-validation, the L-curve, or a noise-level-based discrepancy principle) is a modeling decision with consequences for spatial resolution.

\begin{proposition}[Bias of ridge regression]
If $y=X\beta+\varepsilon$ with $\E\varepsilon=0$, then $\E\hat\beta_\lambda=\sum_j\phi_j(v_j^\top\beta)v_j$, so the ridge estimator is biased towards zero in every direction with $\lambda>0$, most strongly along small singular values.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $u_j^\top y=\sigma_jv_j^\top\beta+u_j^\top\varepsilon$ into the filter-factor formula and take expectations.
\end{proof}

\section{Tensor rank and uniqueness}
For a third-order tensor, the \emph{CP rank} is the smallest $K$ for which an exact decomposition $\sum_{k\le K}a_k\circ b_k\circ c_k$ exists. Unlike matrix rank, tensor rank is hard to compute, can depend on whether the field is $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, and the set of tensors of rank at most $K$ need not be closed, so a ``best rank-$K$ approximation'' may fail to exist (factor norms diverge while the fit improves). In exchange, CP decompositions are often unique up to scaling and permutation, which matrix factorizations never are.

\begin{theorem}[Kruskal, stated]
Let $k_A$ denote the largest number $k$ such that every $k$ columns of $A$ are linearly independent (the Kruskal rank), and similarly $k_B,k_C$. If $k_A+k_B+k_C\ge2K+2$, the rank-$K$ CP decomposition is unique up to permutation and scaling of the components.
\end{theorem}
Uniqueness is the mathematical reason tensor methods are popular for neuron $\times$ time $\times$ trial data: a matrix factorization $X\approx AB^\top$ can be replaced by $(AM)(BM^{-\top})^\top$ for any invertible $M$, so its factors are not individually interpretable without further constraints, whereas a CP decomposition satisfying Kruskal's condition is.

\begin{workedexample}[A two-electrode inverse problem]
Two electrodes record $y=Hx$ from three candidate sources with
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.5&0.2\\0.2&0.5&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The rows are independent, so $\rank H=2$ and, by rank--nullity, $\ker H$ is one-dimensional. A spanning vector is the cross product of the two rows, $(0.4,-0.96,0.4)^\top$, i.e.\ $z\propto(5,-12,5)^\top$; indeed $Hz=(5-6+1,\;1-6+5)^\top=0$. Any source pattern can be altered by a multiple of $z$ without changing the recordings: a strong middle source can be traded for two outer sources of opposite sign. A minimum-norm estimate removes the $z$ component; a sparsity prior may instead drive one source to zero. The data alone cannot decide between them.
\end{workedexample}

\item Exhibit two distinct $x\in\mathbb R^2$ satisfying $[1\ 1]x=2$.
\item Explain why a CP decomposition's factors may be arbitrarily rescaled.
\item Describe what you would need to claim that a reduced-rank relationship reflects direct anatomical connectivity.
\item Show that $XX^+X=X$ and $X^+XX^+=X^+$.
\item Show that the filter factors $\phi_j$ are decreasing in $\lambda$ and compute $\lambda$ for which $\phi_j=1/2$.
\item Verify that $(a\alpha)\circ(b/\alpha)\circ c=a\circ b\circ c$ for any $\alpha\ne0$.
\item For $X=\diag(1,10^{-3})$ and noise of size $10^{-2}$ in each coordinate, compare the errors of the pseudoinverse and of truncated SVD with one component.
\end{exercises}
